Barbell Velocity Privacy Policy

Effective November 10, 2026 (revised the same day)

In short: your lifting videos are analyzed on your iPhone and are never uploaded. If you say yes when the app asks, it sends crash reports and anonymous usage statistics through Google Firebase so I can fix bugs and improve the app. If you say no, it sends nothing. Nothing is used for advertising or tracking, and nothing is sold.

This policy covers the Barbell Velocity iOS app ("the app"), made by Alexander Julian Ty, an independent developer ("I", "me").

Your videos and lifting data

When you choose a video, the app reads it from your photo library and measures bar speed entirely on your device. The results, run history, labels, and the preview clips and thumbnails the app creates are stored only in the app's private storage on your iPhone.

  • Videos and results are not uploaded to me or anyone else.
  • The app only reads the videos you pick. It does not browse the rest of your library.
  • Deleting the app deletes everything it stored. Your original videos stay in your photo library.

Crash reports and usage statistics

The first time you open the app, it asks whether you want to share crash reports and usage statistics. Nothing below is collected unless you choose Share. You can change your answer at any time in the app under Privacy (the shield icon on the main screen); turning it off stops collection immediately.

If you choose Share, the app uses Google's Firebase services (Crashlytics, Firebase Sessions, and Google Analytics for Firebase) to collect the following:

DataWhy
Crash reports: what the app was doing when it crashed, plus device model, iOS version, app version, and timeFinding and fixing bugs
Diagnostic data, such as free memory and storage when a crash happensFinding and fixing bugs
Performance data: device and iOS details, network connection type, and when the app goes to the backgroundGrouping crashes and performance into app sessions
Usage events: which screens are opened, session length, app versionUnderstanding which features are used
App-generated identifiers: a random installation ID created by FirebaseCounting distinct installs and grouping a crash with its session
Approximate location: country or region, estimated by Google from your IP addressUnderstanding where the app is used

This data is not linked to your name, email, or any account, because the app has no accounts. The app does not use Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA), does not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and has advertising features turned off in Firebase.

Google processes this data on my behalf as a service provider, under the Firebase privacy and security terms, which commit Google to protecting it at least as well as this policy does. I share it with no one else.

How long it's kept: crash reports are deleted after 90 days, and analytics event data after 2 months. Deleting and reinstalling the app resets the installation ID.

What I don't do

  • I don't sell or share your data with advertisers or data brokers.
  • I don't use your data for advertising or to track you.
  • I don't collect your name, email, contacts, precise location, or health data.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and I do not knowingly collect information from them.

Your choices and rights

Withdraw consent: open the app, tap the shield icon (Privacy), and turn off Share crash reports and usage stats. Collection stops immediately. Deleting the app also stops it.

Deletion and other requests: because the data is not tied to your identity, I usually can't single out which records are yours, and they are deleted automatically on the schedule above. For any privacy question or request, including deletion or access requests under laws such as the GDPR or CCPA, email me and I'll help as far as I'm able.

Changes to this policy

Future versions may add optional features such as cloud backup of videos. If they do, I'll update this policy before the feature ships, and the app will ask for your consent before uploading anything. The effective date at the top always shows the latest version.
